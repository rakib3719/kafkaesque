# Kafkaesque — Exhibition Design Prompt

Design and build a cinematic, interactive web exhibition that helps a first-time visitor *feel and understand* Franz Kafka's ideas without turning the site into a biography, a bookshop, a portfolio, or a generic dark-themed museum.

## Core thesis

Kafka's rooms, offices, courts, doors, and castles are not destinations to reproduce literally. They are metaphorical pressure systems. The exhibition must turn these pressures into clear visual experiences:

1. **The body** — transformation changes how society sees a person.
2. **The law** — judgment can exist before an explanation.
3. **The system** — a process can be everywhere while its centre remains unreachable.
4. **The threshold** — permission can become a life-long form of waiting.

## Art direction

- Create one coherent, minimal, monumental visual world: ink-black, paper bone, dusty stone, and one institutional red.
- Use large editorial typography, empty space, fine technical labels, and a single strong visual gesture per chapter.
- Make motion purposeful and restrained. GSAP scroll reveals should expose an idea, never decorate it.
- Avoid ornate gold frames, game-dashboard UI, fantasy-library imagery, fake bureaucracy forms, confetti, generic cards, excessive hover effects, and a hero portrait of Kafka.
- The visitor should feel they are entering an exhibition, not browsing a landing page.

## Narrative architecture

1. **Orientation** — “This is not a biography.” Introduce Kafka as an architecture of pressure.
2. **The atlas** — a visual map of the four conditions; selecting one reveals the associated work, question, and explanation.
3. **Three case studies** — *The Metamorphosis*, *The Trial*, and *The Castle* as evidence for the conditions, not as product cards.
4. **Reading compass** — give the visitor an honest, simple route into the works based on the pressure they recognise.
5. **Closing frame** — end on a quiet, cinematic question rather than a marketing CTA.

## Success test

Within one minute, a visitor who has never read Kafka should understand why “Kafkaesque” means alienation, opaque authority, impossible procedures, and waiting before a door that may never open.
